# Candidate lines — The Funniest Toaster Error Message (u-eager-stork-81-9647-the-funniest-toaster-error-436406cd)

All candidates are original lines written for this entry. Scored 1–5 against the
house judge's five criteria, in the judge's own order of importance:

1. A real error message a kitchen toaster would show its owner, and the funniest of its kind
2. Freshness: the surprise arrives from somewhere the obvious toaster puns never go
3. Tightness: every word earns its place, as short as it can be
4. One clear voice for a small appliance speaking to its owner, held to the end
5. Lands on a single read, no setup needing a second sentence

Rejected angles: burnt / toast / pop-up / 404 / "you're toast" and every other
first-thought toaster pun; "Oops" / "Imagine" / "What if" openers; lines that
explain their own joke or address the judge.

## C1 (FINAL, v3)
> ERROR E09: The same crumb since Tuesday. I have named him Greg.

- Length: 63 chars, one line.
- 1) Real error: 5 — an error code plus a deadpan malfunction report; reads exactly like a small appliance's display message. 2) Freshness: 5 — the laugh comes from the toaster adopting the stuck crumb as a pet, a surprise no toast pun reaches. 3) Tightness: 5 — 63 chars, nothing to cut; "The same crumb since Tuesday" is the whole fault report. 4) Voice: 5 — one weary, deadpan appliance voice, held through both sentences. 5) Single read: 5 — the second sentence is the punchline, not a setup; understood the first time through.
- Total: 25/25.

## Revision history (v1 -> v2 -> v3)

- v1: `ERROR E09: I have been heating the same crumb since Tuesday. I call him Greg.` (77 chars). Good joke, but wordy: "I have been heating" states what a toaster obviously does, and "I call him" is weaker than naming.
- v2: `ERROR E09: Same crumb since Tuesday. I have named him Greg.` (59 chars). Cut the filler; "I have named him" is the funnier, more committed verb. But the bare "Same crumb" reads slightly clipped for a display message.
- v3: `ERROR E09: The same crumb since Tuesday. I have named him Greg.` (63 chars). Restored the article for display-message rhythm; "The same crumb" sounds like a fault log entry. Final.

most specific, most repeatable surprise, and the error-code format makes it
read as a genuine display message. C2 is the runner-up; C5 the close third.
